# Win MOLTEN by building inside the headset

Spatial's strongest pitch tonight is the in-headset Studio, not the marketplace. MOLTEN scores five equal 1–5 criteria and breaks ties on "It works in space". It states plainly that business model, market size, slides and pitch polish are **not scored**, and its lowest score for "A new way to create" goes to "a familiar 2D tool moved into a headset", which is exactly how a component website reads ([MOLTEN agents.md](https://moltenhack.com/agents.md)). The white space is real. Across roughly 40 XR libraries, editors, marketplaces and AI builders checked, **none combines** a copy-paste, source-owned XR component registry with building a logic-wired app inside the headset that launches to a URL and an MCP that serves components and saved layouts to outside coding agents. The most dangerous neighbour is **Meta's Immersive Web SDK (IWSDK) 1.0**, whose launch post went up on 14 Oct 2026. It ships an MCP server, agent skills and an "Agentic Scene Composer" on the same pmndrs/uikit foundation Spatial uses. Its editor, though, runs in a managed desktop browser over scene files, and it has no component registry ([Meta](https://developers.meta.com/vr/blog/immersive-web-sdk-1-0/); [IWSDK guide](https://iwsdk.dev/guides/09-native-scene-editor.html)). "Why not just use Claude Code?" has a measured answer: agents now write 3D code that *runs* but rarely code that *behaves*. The best of nine frontier models covered only **27.8%** of hidden behavioural checks on a 2,026-task Three.js benchmark ([arXiv 2606.01869](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01869)). The market cuts both ways. VR headset shipments fell **28% in 2025**, but Meta reports **1M+ monthly WebXR users** and is paying developers for hands-first apps ahead of its $1,299.99 VR Glasses in spring 2027. The honest frame is "web developers entering XR as input goes hands-first", not "VR is booming". The credibility risks are specific. **196 of 217** catalogue entries are marked ready, and the last visual QA rated **4 of 150 broken and 30 ugly**. The "live code" comes from deterministic codegen of the layout. Cross-device claims are untested beyond Quest.

## The rubric scores the headset demo and ignores the business

MOLTEN is "a one-day hackathon for prototyping the tools of spatial creation", and the organizers frame the problem in words Spatial can borrow: "Spatial consumption shipped… but authoring is still 2D: Blender and a monitor, Unity and a mouse" ([MOLTEN agents.md](https://moltenhack.com/agents.md)). Judging happens live at the team's table from 6:30 PM. Judges are told to "have it running, hand them the headset", and their checklist includes "Let them use their own body" ([MOLTEN judges.js](https://moltenhack.com/judges.js)). The demo video must show the thing working, and **"A video with slides will not be judged."** The organizers say "under a minute is plenty", while the portal checklist asks for a backup video of "two minutes or less" ([agents.md](https://moltenhack.com/agents.md); [portal app.js](https://moltenhack.com/app.js)). The same checklist asks teams to state "What works and what is stubbed". The portal rules add that "Judges care far more about honesty than purity, and they find out anyway" ([portal api/bootstrap](https://moltenhack.com/api/bootstrap)).

## Meta's IWSDK 1.0 ships the engine and agent loop, not hands-first building or a shelf

IWSDK is open source, built on Three.js with an entity-component system (ECS), and was MIT-licensed at its Connect 2025 launch ([GitHub](https://github.com/facebook/immersive-web-sdk)). The 1.0 launch post (14 Oct 2026; npm release notes date the build to 24 Sep) says **every new project includes an MCP server and agent skills**. Through them the agent can "view the scene, move the headset, controllers, and hands" and "search the real IWSDK API instead of guessing". The post also introduces an "Agentic Scene Composer, a visual editor your agent can drive", with gaze-and-pinch for Meta VR Glasses ([Meta](https://developers.meta.com/vr/blog/immersive-web-sdk-1-0/); [MIXED](https://mixed-news.com/en/meta-immersive-web-sdk-1-0-gaze-and-pinch-webxr/)). Its spatial UI is authored in UIKitML on **pmndrs/uikit**, with a shadcn-based Default Kit and a Horizon Kit, both installed as packages ([IWSDK Spatial UI](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/documentation/web/iwsdk-guide-spatial-ui/)). The AI tooling adds RAG over 3,337 code chunks and six bundled Claude Code skills, but the docs describe **no component registry or UI component search** ([IWSDK AI tooling](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/documentation/web/iwsdk-ai-assisted-dev-tooling)). Meta claims it rebuilt Project Flowerbed, originally tens of thousands of lines, in about 15 hours, but published no methodology ([Road to VR](https://roadtovr.com/meta-immersive-web-ai-agent-toolkit-2026/)).

The question the earlier research left open now has an answer. IWSDK's own editor guide says "the managed Playwright browser… owns the editor wrapper" and "scene files are the authoring source; the managed editor is the live visual surface for selection, transforms, hierarchy… and human fine adjustment". It never mentions editing in VR or with hands ([IWSDK native scene editor guide](https://iwsdk.dev/guides/09-native-scene-editor.html)). So Spatial can truthfully say IWSDK's composer is a desktop-browser editor that you share with an agent, while Spatial is authored in the room with your hands. IWSDK's other gaps are just as concrete: its kits are npm packages rather than owned source, it uses vanilla Three.js plus ECS rather than React Three Fiber, and its 1.0 messaging centres on Quest and Meta's glasses. The threat is equally concrete. Meta has distribution, and because both stacks share uikit, adding a component registry to its existing MCP would be a short step. The safest framing is complementary: Spatial is the hands-first studio and the tested component layer that sits on top of engines like IWSDK, not a rival engine.

Three days before, on 8 Oct, Meta published a **v0-by-Vercel template for IWSDK**. You describe the experience, v0 writes the code, and you deploy in one click to an HTTPS URL that opens in Quest Browser or packages as a Horizon Store app ([Meta](https://developers.meta.com/vr/blog/build-for-meta-vr-with-v0-by-vercel/)). That template already covers Spatial's "Launch to URL" step. What it lacks is spatial placement and any UI kit, and it is authored by prompting at a desk.

PatchWorld deserves special attention because the broad competitor sweep missed it. It is the one product where people really do wire knobs and faders by hand in a headset to make something that works, with MIDI, OSC and Ableton Link ([Mixed News](https://mixed-news.com/en/patchworld-trippy-jam-sessions-with-friends-for-meta-quest-pc-vr/)). Its output lives inside PatchWorld. Spatial's output is a URL anyone can open plus source the developer owns, and that distinction is the whole argument.

Telerik retired its paid Unity XR UI kit in April 2024 ([Telerik](https://www.telerik.com/unity-xr-ui)). Closed, hosted XR tools and closed XR UI kits have both failed to sustain themselves, which favours shadcn's open copy-paste model.

That 2D model is proven. shadcn/ui has 125.8k GitHub stars ([GitHub](https://github.com/shadcn-ui/ui)), and its official MCP installs from any registry declared in `components.json` for Claude Code, Cursor, Copilot and Codex ([shadcn MCP](https://ui.shadcn.com/docs/mcp)). 21st.dev (YC W26) charges $6–8/month for unlimited MCP component retrieval, but its own YC page gives inconsistent user counts (1M, 1.4M and 2M), and its launch focus has drifted to an agents SDK ([YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/21st); [21st.dev pricing](https://21st.dev/pricing)). That drift is a caution that a component marketplace alone may not be the business. A cheap, high-leverage move follows: publish Spatial's registry in shadcn's registry format, so existing shadcn clients can pull XR components with no new tooling.

## Agents write XR code that runs, rarely code that behaves

The strongest answer to "why not ask Claude Code?" is a gap between code that *runs* and code that *works*, and the benchmarks measure it. WorldCoder-Bench (June 2026) runs 2,026 expert-curated Three.js tasks in a sandboxed browser and checks hidden behavioural contracts against runtime state. "The best system reaches only **27.8%** verification coverage on WorldCoder-Core and 19.9% on WorldCoder-Robust" across nine frontier models. The authors trace the failures to "state-schema drift and broken interaction chains rather than missing scene elements" ([arXiv 2606.01869](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01869)). In other words, models get the objects into the scene and break the interaction layer, the poke, pinch and grab behaviour that Spatial's components package. Grading these worlds automatically is unreliable as well. In WorldBench, a vision judge and a code judge disagreed on 32% of required items, and the code judge gave full credit for four of five deliberately removed features ([arXiv 2610.10622](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10622)).

Google's Vibe Coding XR paper is the most useful single data point, because it argues both sides. Gemini 3.1 Pro reached **95.5% Pass@1**, but "pass" meant zero-error execution in headless Chromium, not correct or comfortable interaction. The rate started near **70%** and climbed to 95% only after six months of hardening the framework. Early errors came from framework bugs and "hallucination of non-existing or deprecated APIs", and faster models stumbled on "precise hand-interaction logic" ([arXiv 2603.24591](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24591v1)). Google credits template and sample source code with reducing hallucination, and says XR evaluation "typically requires hands-on, on-device testing" ([Google Research](https://research.google/blog/vibe-coding-xr-accelerating-ai-xr-prototyping-with-xr-blocks-and-gemini/)). That is measured evidence that curated, tested building blocks raise agent success on XR, and the gain came from the parts, not the model. It is also evidence that platform owners will build their own curated layers. In 2D, Storybook claims its MCP gives 12.8% better component usage, runs 2.76× faster and uses 27% fewer tokens, but it has not published its method ([Storybook](https://storybook.js.org/blog/storybook-mcp-for-react/)).

What a "correct" XR component encodes is concrete. Meta specifies hand targets of at least 48dp (about 22 mm), spaced at least 12 mm apart, with touch UI placed 42–46 cm from the user ([Meta hands guidance](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/design/hands-quickstart-guide/); [Hands UI best practices](https://developers.meta.com/vr/design/hands-ui-best-practices/)). Target size measurably changes neck and shoulder load ([Ergonomics](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00140139.2024.2396038)). **No published study measures whether LLM-generated XR UI meets these constants.** The honest claim is "unmeasured, and by Google's and Meta's own account only verifiable on a device", not "LLMs can't do it".

The counter-arguments are strong and should be conceded. Agentic tool access closes gaps fast: one agent's 3D reconstruction score rose from 0.725 to 0.927 once it could drive Blender through MCP ([3DHarnessBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.06535)). The ergonomic constants are public, so a good AGENTS.md could encode them. The defensible part is therefore **tuned, device-tested feel**: press depth, hit-slop, debounce and cross-device quirks, not the constants themselves. One more twist cuts both ways. R3F, @react-three/xr and uikit have far fewer training examples than Tailwind and shadcn, which makes curated source more valuable as context and also more likely to be "fixed" by an agent that doesn't recognise it.

### Blender MCP makes the things; Spatial makes them touchable and ships them

"Claude drives Blender" is mainstream. The community MCP for Blender has about **31.4k stars** and was renamed on 16 Sep 2026. It executes arbitrary Python over a socket with "no authentication or encryption" ([GitHub](https://github.com/ahujasid/blender-mcp)). Blender Lab's official server (v1.0.0, 27 Apr 2026) offers 26 tools and no 3D generation, and it will "execute LLM generated code in Blender without any guards in place" ([StraySpark](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/official-blender-mcp-server-comparison-2026)). Anthropic's Blender connector, launched 28 Apr 2026, helps artists "analyze and debug entire Blender scenes" ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-for-creative-work)). All of these are content-creation tools for assets and scenes. None has a concept of poke or pinch affordances, XR session state, or a deployable web app.

That leaves a clean division of labour. Blender, Claude-in-Blender or Kaedim produces a GLB. Spatial's Studio imports it as an editable object with a grab handle; GLB import shipped at 2:20 PM ([LOG.md](/Users/ek/molten/LOG.md)). The object gets wired to values and launches as a URL. The return trip, a Studio layout exported through three.js `GLTFExporter` for an art pass in Blender, is plausible from the exporter's documented options ([three.js](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/GLTFExporter.html)), but it is **untested** and should not be claimed.

The two MCPs also run in opposite directions. Blender MCP lets an agent *drive a tool*. Spatial's MCP lets an agent *pull tested source into the developer's own repo*. Its `add_component` copies one `.tsx` into `components/spatial/` and "never overwrites unless `overwrite: true`" ([mcp/README.md](/Users/ek/molten/mcp/README.md)). A server that hands over readable files has a far smaller attack surface than one that runs unguarded Python. Being listed is no moat, though: the official MCP registry held **35,491 servers** on 24 Sep 2026, and **78.7%** of those listed for 90+ days had shipped nothing in the past 90 days ([Oktopost](https://www.oktopost.com/blog/mcp-registry-legacy-servers/)). A maintained server attached to a living, tested catalogue is what stands out.

## Demand comes from web developers and hands-first input, not headset growth

The headset numbers do not support a growth pitch. Counterpoint counts global VR headset shipments **down 28% in 2025** and **down 18% year over year in Q2 2026**, while smart glasses rose 215% ([Counterpoint](https://www.counterpointresearch.com/insight/global-xr-ar-vr-headsets-market-share-quarterly); [Counterpoint tracker](https://counterpointresearch.com/en/reports/global-smart-glasses-country-shipments-tracker-q2-2026)). Reality Labs lost **$4.62B on $431M revenue in Q2 2026** ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/articles/meta-reality-labs-lost-4-144126133.html)). Meta raised Quest prices in April ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/16/meta-raises-quest-3-and-quest-3s-prices-due-to-ram-shortage/)), showed no new Quest at Connect 2026, and in January cut more than 10% of Reality Labs and shut Workrooms and Quest for Business ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/01/16/meta-stop-selling-quest-headsets-businesses-discontinue-multiple-vr-features/)). IDC's forecast of 13.6M display-less smart glasses in 2026 is volume with no spatial UI at all ([IDC](https://www.idc.com/resource-center/blog/smart-glasses-surge-the-xr-market-is-rewriting-its-own-rules/)). The pro-XR developer pool is small too. In the GDC 2026 survey, 8% of 2,300+ respondents had worked on VR, AR or MR in the past year, and 82% of those 94 specialists built for Meta ([GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gdc-the-state-of-the-game-industry-fcf)).

The web side tells a different story. Meta says **over one million monthly users** reach WebXR content on Quest ([Road to VR](https://roadtovr.com/meta-immersive-web-ai-agent-toolkit-2026/)). WebXR PWAs can be listed in the Horizon Store with in-app purchases ([Meta PWA docs](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/documentation/web/pwa-overview/)). Chrome on Android XR runs WebXR with hand input on by default ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/develop/xr/web)), and Safari on visionOS has had WebXR on by default since visionOS 2, VR only ([WebKit](https://webkit.org/blog/15162/introducing-natural-input-for-webxr-in-apple-vision-pro/)).

The sharpest, dated demand signal is the **shift in input**. Meta's VR Glasses ($1,299.99, spring 2027) are built around hands and gaze, with controllers sold separately ([VR.org](https://vr.org/meta-connect-2026)). Meta's $1M VR Start competition requires apps "fully usable with hands, end-to-end", and submissions close **18 Nov 2026**; one line in the same coverage cites $1.5M across 32 prizes ([UploadVR](https://www.uploadvr.com/meta-launches-1-million-competition-for-hands-first-apps-ahead-of-vr-glasses/)). Google warns that controller-centric WebXR apps need code changes for hand input ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/develop/xr/web)). Retrofitting for hands is UI and interaction work, which is exactly what tuned components address.

The direct pain evidence is indirect and should be presented that way. Every platform owner ships official spatial UI kits, Meta several times over ([Meta ISDK v69](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/blog/interaction-sdk-isdk-developers-unity-unreal-v69/)), which implies developers were rebuilding buttons and sliders. The CHI 2020 Best Paper found eight barriers across prototyping, implementation and testing among 21 AR/VR creators ([Ashtari et al.](https://hci.cs.umanitoba.ca/Publications/details/creating-augmented-and-virtual-reality-applications-current-practices-chall)). A 2025 paper notes "a noticeable lack of unified and comprehensive design guidelines for VR UI design" ([arXiv 2508.09358](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.09358v1)). **No survey quantifies how much XR development time goes to UI**, and that is the biggest hole in the demand story.

Market-size signals are mostly analogies, and none of them should appear on stage because market size is not scored. On the web, the `shadcn` CLI sees about **3.03M weekly npm downloads** ([Snyk](https://security.snyk.io/package/npm/shadcn)), shadcn usage doubled from 20% to 42% in State of React 2024 ([State of React](https://state-of-react-2024.onrender.com/en-US/libraries/component-libraries)), and v0 claims over 4M users ([Vercel community](https://community.vercel.com/t/news-cache-2026-02-09/33058)). The XR-in-React pool is far smaller: `@pmndrs/xr` shows about **5,000** weekly downloads and `@react-three/uikit` about **3,700** ([npm](https://npmjs.com/package/@pmndrs/xr); [npm](https://npmjs.com/package/@react-three/uikit)), so it is roughly 600 times smaller than shadcn's audience. Enterprise immersive-training estimates for 2025 range from **$5.79B to $17.91B** depending on who defines the category ([TBRC via GII](https://giikorea.co.kr/report/tbrc2009610-extended-reality-training-global-market-report.html); [Meticulous Research](https://www.meticulousresearch.com/product/immersive-training-market-6237)). A threefold spread is a reason not to cite either number.

## Conclusion

The rubric and the competitive map point the same way. The in-headset Studio is both Spatial's main scoring asset and the one pillar no competitor has shipped. Meta now gives away the engine, the agent loop and a desktop editor, and v0 gives away launch-to-URL. Spatial's durable position is therefore as a *layer that engines and agents consume*, not as an engine. Concretely, that means hands-first authoring plus tested parts in a registry format that shadcn, IWSDK and R3F projects can all pull from. Google's jump from 70% to 95% is the best measured evidence that curated parts make agents better at XR, and the same evidence shows platform owners will curate for themselves. The cheapest way to turn Spatial's thesis into evidence after tonight is a small on-device eval: Spatial components against raw Claude Code output, scored on Meta's 22 mm, 12 mm and 42–46 cm specs and on hand-task success.

The demand story also changed under scrutiny. A headset-growth argument fails on the numbers. A *change in input* argument holds, and it carries dates: hands-only competition entries by 18 Nov, VR Glasses in spring 2027, and Google telling controller-era WebXR apps to change. Tonight that means one sentence, a knob grabbed in the first three seconds, and a plain statement of what is stubbed. Honesty is the cheapest point on the rubric to earn.
